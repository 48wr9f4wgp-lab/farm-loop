## 2. Final benchmark roles for the Commercial Proof phase

### A. Hay Day — Mobile farm loop / friction / readability
Current public App Store signal:
- Japan rating: 4.4 / ~77K ratings
- free + IAP
- August 2026 update explicitly reduces friction: faster truck-order flow, clearer help locations, continued reward-road rewards

Transferable strengths:
- player can understand what is ready without opening management screens
- short repeated actions
- farm itself remains emotionally valuable as a place, not just a resource table
- long-term decoration/customization supports attachment

2026 player-voice risk signals:
- event overload can turn a relaxing farm into a job
- repeated timed obligations create FOMO
- large order/resource requirements can feel unreasonable

Farm Loop decision:
ADOPT: quick work feedback, obvious ready state, optional goals, farm/world attachment.
REJECT: overlapping mandatory events, high-frequency FOMO, quantity inflation as difficulty.

### B. Japanese Rural Life Adventure — Japanese rural identity / seasonality
Current public App Store signal:
- US rating: 4.9 / ~19K ratings
- strong Japanese countryside identity
- farming, cooking, wildlife, village repair, mountain exploration, annual customs and seasons

Transferable strengths:
- cultural specificity is itself a product value
- seasonal rituals make calendar progression emotionally meaningful
- mountain/village activities make the game larger than the farm plot

Player-voice risk signals:
- repeated fishing/selling for money can become grind
- collection chores can become excessive
- point-and-click movement/tapping can become tedious
- overly linear gating can reduce cozy freedom

Farm Loop decision:
ADOPT: snow-country specificity, annual anticipation, mountain + village as core pillars.
REJECT: one-resource grind, high tap-count traversal, long mandatory collection lists, rigid A->B->C progression.

### C. Stardew Valley — Premium value / pacing / progression
Current public App Store signal:
- US rating: 4.8 / ~64K ratings
- US price shown: $4.99
- no IAP
- 50+ hours marketed content
- seasonal farming + relationships + exploration + artisan production

Transferable strengths:
- strong sense that there is always something worthwhile to do next
- progression can be self-directed without heavy LiveOps pressure
- seasons create anticipation
- the world, characters, production and exploration reinforce each other

Recent player-voice signals:
- charm/heart and pacing are repeatedly cited as reasons it remains a reference point
- players value accomplishment without anxiety and freedom to progress at their own pace
- mobile-specific input conflicts can still damage otherwise excellent gameplay

Farm Loop decision:
ADOPT: premium-value mindset, self-directed progression, season anticipation, interconnected systems.
REJECT: copying content breadth; Farm Loop must reach value with a smaller, denser system set.

### D. Heartopia — 2026 cozy visual / customization reference only
Current public store signal:
- US App Store rating: 4.6 / ~33K ratings
- Google Play Japan: 4.4 / ~113K reviews
- free + IAP
- multiplayer life sim; large customization/hobby scope
- iOS package shown around 3.9 GB

Transferable strengths:
- high contemporary visual expectations for cozy mobile products
- character/avatar/world presentation creates immediate emotional appeal
- multiple hobbies make the world feel inhabited

Risk signals:
- FOMO/gacha pressure can conflict with cozy positioning
- players can still hit content fatigue despite breadth
- mobile performance complaints include freezing/stutter
- production scope is far beyond Farm Loop's intended small-team scale

Farm Loop decision:
ADOPT ONLY: visual quality bar, scene richness, customization readability.
REJECT: gacha economy, FOMO content cadence, multiplayer/live-service scope, multi-gigabyte asset burden.

### E. Township — long-term meta / anti-scope benchmark
Current public App Store signal:
- Japan rating: 4.6 / ~350K ratings
- free + IAP, including randomized items
- farm + city + match-3 + competition + frequent events

Transferable strengths:
- visible long-term transformation
- production feeds a larger place players care about
- large progression horizon

Risk signals:
- recent community complaints focus on match-3 difficulty, resource depletion and blocked progression
- reward/removal changes can damage trust
- LiveOps complexity is incompatible with Farm Loop's intended production model

Farm Loop decision:
ADOPT: visible place growth and long-term horizon.
REJECT: unrelated secondary game gating, progression blockers, LiveOps dependency, content treadmill.

## 3. Cross-title player-voice clusters

### Positive Cluster P1 — "My place"
Players remain attached when the farm/town/home is emotionally valuable even without a task.

Farm Loop requirement:
The satoyama must visibly become more beautiful, alive and personal as progression increases.

### Positive Cluster P2 — Progress without pressure
Strong cozy responses appear when players can choose what to do while still seeing a clear next opportunity.

Farm Loop requirement:
Always show a useful next action, but do not punish ignoring it.

### Positive Cluster P3 — Seasonal anticipation
Season change is not just a stat change; it creates a reason to continue.

Farm Loop requirement:
Each season must alter visuals, available discoveries, requests and at least one meaningful routine.

### Positive Cluster P4 — Cultural/world specificity
Generic farming is replaceable. Specific place/culture/routine can become identity.

Farm Loop requirement:
Snow-country satoyama, sansai, mushroom logs, mountain work, snow, village and ecology stay central.

### Negative Cluster N1 — Cozy turns into work
Too many simultaneous events, repeated check-ins, energy/FOMO and quantity inflation create churn risk.

Farm Loop hard rule:
No mandatory high-frequency LiveOps treadmill in initial product.

### Negative Cluster N2 — Repetition without decision
Fishing/collecting/tapping the same action to fund progression becomes grind.

Farm Loop hard rule:
Repeated work must compress, automate, batch, branch, or create a meaningful choice before repetition becomes the dominant activity.

### Negative Cluster N3 — Artificial progression blockers
Unrelated minigames, resource starvation or strict linear gates damage agency.

Farm Loop hard rule:
No system may stop the core loop unless the block itself creates an intentional, recoverable game decision.

### Negative Cluster N4 — Monetization breaks the fantasy
FOMO/gacha/pay pressure is particularly visible when the product promises peace/coziness.

Farm Loop direction:
Premium-first remains the leading monetization hypothesis for Commercial Proof. Do not implement monetization until the Proof-of-Fun gate passes.

### Negative Cluster N5 — Mobile friction is disproportionately costly
Touch conflicts, excessive tapping, small targets, freeze/stutter can negate strong content.

Farm Loop requirement:
Keep smartphone-first physical-device QA as a Hard Gate.

## 4. Farm Loop product rules locked by this pass

1. Farm Loop is not a generic crop-production game.
2. Farm Loop is not a Township-scale LiveOps game.
3. The first commercial promise is: "restore and circulate a Japanese snow-country satoyama."
4. The farm, mountain, village and seasons are one loop; none should feel like an unrelated menu.
5. No energy system in the Proof-of-Fun slice.
6. No forced ads, gacha, battle pass or timed FOMO in the Proof-of-Fun slice.
7. Batch repetitive chores before adding more content.
8. Every month/season must create anticipation, not only bookkeeping.
9. Visible world growth is mandatory progression feedback.
10. Mobile input/performance issues outrank content additions.

## 5. Proof-of-Fun hypotheses

H1: Players who complete the first circulation loop will voluntarily advance another month.
H2: Players will describe the game using at least two differentiation pillars: snow-country / ecology loop / mountain work / village / seasons.
H3: Players will identify at least one action as satisfying, not merely understandable.
H4: Players will want to see the next season without being pushed by a timed reward.
H5: No single repeated task will be described as necessary grinding within the first 15 minutes.

If H1/H2/H3 fail in external testing, do not solve it by adding content. Redesign the core experience.
